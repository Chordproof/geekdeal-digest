How We Verify Game Prices (And Why Most Deal Sites Don't)

Most deal sites republish whatever a store feed sends them. That is how a game that has cost $2 for three years ends up with a "90% off" badge. Here is the four-step process we run before any price reaches this site.

GeekDeal Digest · 6 min read

There is a structural problem with how game deal sites work, and almost nobody talks about it.

A store publishes a sale. The store's affiliate network pushes that sale into a feed. The feed lands in a database. A deal site renders the database. At no point in that chain does anyone ask a simple question: is this actually a good price?

The answer is usually no. A "90% off" badge means the current price is 90% below the list price the publisher set — and publishers set list prices. A game that launched at $59.99, dropped to $19.99 permanently two years ago, and is now on sale for $5.99 will show as "90% off" against the original $59.99. Technically true. Practically misleading.

We built GeekDeal Digest to fix that. Here is exactly how.

Step 1 — Pull from the store, not from a reseller

Every price on this site starts with a live pull from the store's own feed. Not a third-party aggregator, not a cached snapshot, not a screenshot someone took last week.

This matters because aggregators introduce lag. A deal that expired four hours ago can still appear live on an aggregator that syncs twice a day. When you click through, you land on a full-price page and the deal site has already collected the click.

We pull directly. If the store says the price is $5.69, we record $5.69 — with the timestamp of the moment we read it.

Step 2 — Check the sale price against the store's own list price

This is the step most sites skip. We take two numbers from the same source, at the same moment:

  • The sale price — what the store is charging right now.
  • The list price — what the store itself says the game normally costs.

Both come from the store. We do not use historical averages, we do not use "lowest price ever recorded", and we do not use a publisher's suggested retail price from three years ago. The comparison is between two numbers the store is publishing today.

If a store does not publish a list price, we do not publish a discount percentage. We show the price and nothing else. A missing number is more honest than an invented one.

Step 3 — Calculate the discount from those two numbers

The percentage is arithmetic, not editorial:

discount = ((list price − sale price) / list price) × 100

No rounding up. No "up to 90% off" framing. If the math gives 89.4%, we show 89%, not 90%.

This sounds pedantic until you compare two sites side by side. One shows "90% off" on a $5.99 game. The other shows "89% off" on the same game. The second one is telling you the truth.

Step 4 — Publish with a timestamp, and let it age visibly

Every deal card carries the exact time we verified the price. Not "today", not "recently" — the timestamp.

Then the timestamp does something most sites would never allow: it ages in public.

Age of verification Marker What it means
Under 24 hours Fresh Price confirmed today. Safe to buy.
24 to 48 hours Aging Price was correct yesterday. Re-check before buying.
Over 48 hours Stale Price may have changed. Confirm on the store page.

Most deal sites hide the age of their data because stale data still earns clicks. We show it because a click that leads to a dead deal costs you money and costs us trust.

What we do not do

Being clear about the limits matters as much as describing the process.

  • We do not test games. We are not a review outlet. When we say a game is worth $5.69, we are talking about price relative to the market, not about whether you will enjoy it.
  • We do not track historical lows. A price history chart requires months of continuous data. We are building that, but we do not have it yet, and we will not pretend otherwise.
  • We do not cover every store. If a store is not in our pipeline, its deals are not on this site. That is a coverage gap, not a judgment.
  • We do not guarantee prices. Stores change prices without notice. Our timestamp tells you when we last looked — it cannot tell you what the price is right now.

Why this matters for you

A deal site's only real product is trust. If you click a "90% off" link and land on a full-price page, you stop trusting the next link. Three bad clicks and you never come back.

The verification process above is slower and more expensive than republishing a feed. It also means we publish fewer deals — some weeks, far fewer. But every deal we do publish has a number behind it that we can defend.

That is the whole proposition. Not the biggest list. The one you can check.

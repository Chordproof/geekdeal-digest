About

Deals you can trust, or no deal at all

GeekDeal exists because most deal sites optimise for clicks, not for whether you actually saved money.

We cover gaming and geek hardware for one market: the United States. Prices, taxes, storefronts and stock differ from country to country, so a listing that is a bargain in one market is frequently a bad buy in another. We only publish prices we can verify on a US storefront, in US dollars, with the timestamp of the check attached.

How verification works

Every deal on this site goes through the same short process. We open the listing at the retailer, confirm the price is what the page claims, check that the item is in stock, and then stamp the card with the exact date and time of that check. That timestamp is the most important thing on the page — it tells you how much to trust the number.

We only show a crossed-out “was” price when we have a logged reference price with its own date. If we have never recorded a baseline for a product, we show the current price alone. We would rather show less information than invent a discount.

What we refuse to do

  • No fake countdowns. A timer that resets when you reload is a lie.
  • No invented percentages. If there is no logged baseline, there is no “-60%” badge.
  • No community voting. We tried it in planning and rejected it — it rewards volume, not accuracy. Instead, every card has a Report outdated button that goes straight to an editor.
  • No paid placement in the deal feed. Sponsored content, when it exists, is labelled and kept separate.

How we make money

Through affiliate commissions. When you buy through a link on GeekDeal, the retailer pays us a small percentage at no extra cost to you. That is the whole model. It does not change which deals we cover, the order they appear in, or whether we tell you a deal is bad. Our full affiliate disclosure explains the details.

Who this is for

People who buy deliberately: the ones who wait for a real drop, who want to know whether a 1440p monitor is a better upgrade than a new GPU, and who are tired of clicking a “deal” that expired last Tuesday. If that sounds like you, the weekly digest is the shortest path.

Editorial independence: affiliate relationships never determine coverage. If a product is not worth the money at its current price, we say so — including when it is sold by a partner.
